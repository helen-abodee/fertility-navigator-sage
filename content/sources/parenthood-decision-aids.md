# Parenthood decision aids and frameworks — source extraction

Research notes for the "I'm not sure about children" reflection path (AYA cancer, fertility
preservation context). All sources retrieved 2026-11-02. Quotes are verbatim from the
document read; nothing is invented. Licensing flags at each source and summarized at the end.

## 1. BEFORE decision aid (Begin Exploring Fertility Options, Risks and Expectations)

- **Authors/publisher:** Speller B, Metcalfe K, Kennedy ED, Facey M, Greenblatt E, Scheer AS,
  Warner E, Joy AA, Wright FC, Baxter NN. Tool by St. Michael's Hospital (Toronto) + Rethink
  Breast Cancer.
- **Paper:** "The 'Begin Exploring Fertility Options, Risks and Expectations' (BEFORE) decision
  aid: development and alpha testing of a fertility tool for premenopausal breast cancer
  patients." BMC Medical Informatics and Decision Making 2019;19:203.
  - URL read: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6819618/ — open access, **paper is CC BY 4.0**.
- **Tool:** https://fertilityaid.rethinkbreastcancer.com/ and full PDF
  https://fertilityaid.rethinkbreastcancer.com/SM_BEFORE_decisionAid_V9.pdf (both read 2026-11-02;
  freely viewable, English and French).
- **Validation:** Developed per IPDAS and the Ottawa Decision Support Framework; alpha tested
  with 10 breast cancer survivors and 7 healthcare providers (usable/acceptable); implementation
  trial registered (NCT03629509). Not an RCT-validated instrument; a development-and-alpha-tested
  decision aid.
- **Structure (32 pages, 12 sections):** about the DA; background (treatments and fertility);
  fertility options before treatment with age-stratified success rates; **parenthood options after
  treatment** (natural pregnancy, frozen embryos/eggs, IVF, egg or embryo donor, surrogacy,
  adoption); timeline; one-page summary; **fertility options exercise** (values clarification);
  questions for providers; what's next; fertility after breast cancer; glossary; sources.

### Fertility options exercise — verbatim (from SM_BEFORE_decisionAid_V9.pdf, pp. 20–23)

Instructions: "This exercise will help you think about what is important to you for each
fertility option." ... "Go through each fertility option and check how important the listed
factors are to you"; "Add in any other factors that are important to you." Each factor is rated
on a two-column check: **"Less Important to Me / More Important to Me."**

Key framing line (equipoise): **"Please note: you can decide on more than one fertility option.
You do not have to make your fertility decision after completing this exercise."**

Items under **"Wait and See"**:
- "There is no cost"
- "I can start my cancer treatment right away"
- "I do not need to inject myself with hormones"
- "I have other parenthood options available to me after treatment"
- "I do not need additional visits to the fertility clinic"
- "This process may add to my emotional stress"
- "I may be unable to have a natural pregnancy after treatment and may regret not keeping my
  options open"
- "Other: ___"

Items under **"Embryo Freezing"** (selection):
- "My children will be genetically related to me and the individual who provided sperm"
- "If I use sperm from my partner, we both must decide what to do with the embryos"
- "I may regret going through this process if I am able to have a natural pregnancy after treatment"
- "There may be cost to create, freeze, and store embryos"
- "My treatment may be delayed 2 or more weeks"
- "I see an embryo as a living person. I may find it hard to decide what to do with them if I do
  not need them after treatment."
- "I may still not be able to have a child after freezing embryos"
- "I will have to travel to the fertility clinic frequently for monitoring and egg collection"

Items under **"Egg Freezing"** (selection):
- "Sperm is not required at the time of my egg freezing"
- "I may regret freezing my eggs if I am able to have a natural pregnancy after treatment"
- "It is not as successful as embryo freezing"
- "I may still not be able to have a child after freezing my eggs"

Items under **"Ovarian Suppression"** (selection):
- "I can start my cancer treatment right away"
- "I will go into menopause and may have symptoms"
- "Research is still ongoing. This option may not work to protect my eggs from chemotherapy."

Closing check: **"Which options am I leaning towards? Check all of the below options that you
would like more information on. Wait and see / Embryo freezing / Egg freezing / Ovarian
suppression / I am not sure yet"** — "I am not sure yet" is an explicit, legitimate answer.

Honest-uncertainty language elsewhere: "your period coming back after treatment does not always
mean you will be able to get pregnant" (p.15); "Research has shown that becoming pregnant after
your cancer diagnosis does not increase your risk of cancer returning" (p.16).

- **Licensing:** The *paper* is CC BY 4.0. The *tool content* is NOT freely reusable: terms of
  use (https://fertilityaid.rethinkbreastcancer.com/terms.php, read 2026-11-02): "Any use,
  copying, distribution, or display of information in the decision aid should be done after
  written approval. Please contact Anne Sorvari (SorvariA@smh.ca) for approval."
  → **Licensing required to reuse item wording; structure/domains can inform our own wording.**

## 2. Peate et al. — fertility decision aid for young women with early breast cancer

- **Authors:** Peate M, Meiser B, Cheah BC, et al. "Making hard choices easier: a prospective,
  multicentre study to assess the efficacy of a fertility-related decision aid in young women
  with early-stage breast cancer." British Journal of Cancer 2012;106(6):1053–1061.
- **URL read:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3304428/ (open access copy).
- **What it is:** C5-sized booklet: information on breast cancer and female fertility, each
  fertility option, plus values clarification worksheets listing advantages and disadvantages of
  each option; participants mark options they are "leaning" towards or against. (The booklet
  itself was distributed via Breast Cancer Network Australia / Cancer Council; full item text is
  in the booklet, not reproduced in the paper.)
- **Validation — the strongest outcome evidence of the oncofertility DAs:** N=120 prospective
  controlled study. Decision aid group had lower decisional conflict (β=−1.51, p=0.004), higher
  fertility knowledge (p=0.02), **less decisional regret at 12 months** (β=−3.73, p=0.031), and
  higher satisfaction with fertility information (p<0.001).
- **Related:** Peate's group later built a web Values Clarification Exercise where "Higher scores
  (e.g., +2) indicated that fertility was considered a priority... lower scores (e.g., −2)
  indicated that fertility was not a priority; 0 was considered neutral" (per JMIR Pediatrics
  2018 parents' DA pilot, https://pediatrics.jmir.org/2018/2/e10463/ — page fetch returned no
  body on 2026-11-02; description from search abstract, treat as secondary until re-read).
- **Licensing:** Conventional journal copyright; booklet © authors/host organizations.
  → **Would require permission to reuse wording. Use as evidence base and for the
  leaning-toward/leaning-against rating pattern (a generic method, not copyrightable).**

## 3. Cancer, Fertility and Me (UK — Jones et al.)

- **Authors:** Jones GL, Moss RH, Darby F, Mahmoodi N, Phillips B, Hughes J, Vogt KS,
  Greenfield DM, Brauten-Smith G, Gath J, Campbell T, Stark D, Velikova G, Snowden JA,
  Baskind E, Mascerenhas M, Yeomanson D, Skull J, Lane S, Bekker HL, Anderson RA.
  Leeds Beckett University; funded by Yorkshire Cancer Research.
- **Paper:** "Cancer, Fertility and Me: Developing and Testing a Novel Fertility Preservation
  Patient Decision Aid..." Frontiers in Oncology 2022;12:896939. URL read:
  https://eprints.leedsbeckett.ac.uk/id/eprint/8691/7/CancerFertilityAndMePV-JONES.pdf —
  **paper is Creative Commons Attribution 4.0** (repository statement).
- **Booklet (v3.0, 40pp) URL read:**
  https://www.leedsbeckett.ac.uk/-/media/files/cancer-fertility-and-me/cfm-decision-aid-12sept19a-web.pdf
  — free to download; **no reuse license printed in the booklet** (standard copyright assumed).
- **Validation:** Built on the Ottawa Decision Support Framework per IPDAS; three-stage
  development (stakeholder co-design n=24; alpha test n=11 patients + 14 HCPs; beta/field test
  n=41 women ages 16–43 across cancers). Preparation for Decision-Making score 82.3/100
  (patients). Qualitative beta results: helped women "make better informed, values-based care
  plans." Not an RCT. Notably includes patients aged 16+ (AYA-relevant) and non-breast cancers.

### Verbatim patient-facing content (booklet v3.0)

Equipoise framing (p.25): **"Some women know for certain whether or not they want to pursue
fertility preservation. Other women find making this decision more difficult. There is no
'right' or 'wrong' decision. The decision depends on what is most important to you at this
time. It depends on the plans you have for your life, after your cancer treatment."**

**"What matters most to you?"** values exercise (p.25). Rate each 1–4 ("1 is not important to
you, 2 is slightly important to you, 3 is quite important to you, and 4 is very important to
you. You can use the numbers more than once."):
- "Making my fertility preservation decision before starting cancer treatment"
- "Starting cancer treatment straight away"
- "Wanting to have my own biological child after my cancer treatment has been completed"
- "Delaying the start of cancer treatment to have fertility preservation"
- "Taking steps to preserve my fertility before cancer treatment"
- "Waiting to see if my fertility comes back naturally after completing cancer treatment"

Option-likelihood exercise (p.26): "How likely are you to consider each of these fertility
preservation treatment options?" — each of No fertility preservation / Egg freezing / Embryo
freezing / Tissue freezing / Ovarian suppression rated on a 5-point scale:
**"Definitely not / Maybe not / Unsure / Maybe / Definitely"** — "Unsure" sits at the midpoint
as a legitimate answer.

Per-option reflection space (repeated for every option): **"What I like about this option /
What worries me about this option."**

Questions to ask the fertility care team (p.24), verbatim:
- "I am not in a relationship but may want a child in future - what are my options?"
- "How long will fertility preservation take and what will be the expected delay to starting my
  cancer treatment?"
- "What are the chances of me being able to have my own children in the future?"
- "Would using any fertility preservation option affect my chances of the cancer coming back?"

Talking-with-others section (p.24) suggests discussing: "Making difficult decisions"; "The
impact of cancer and fertility on your relationships"; "Moral, religious or ethical concerns";
and "Thinking about who you might want to involve in your decision-making - a partner, family
and friends or a nurse or doctor."

Other-paths content: a section on fertility decisions during/after treatment covers donor
eggs/embryos, surrogacy, adoption and fostering, with glossary definitions and signposts
(Donor Conception Network, Adoption UK).

Honest uncertainty, repeated per option: "It is difficult to be certain how likely it is that
egg [embryo / ovarian tissue] freezing..." will result in a baby.

- **Licensing:** Paper CC BY 4.0 (quotable with attribution). Booklet: free to access, no
  stated reuse license → **ask Jones/Leeds Beckett before lifting booklet wording wholesale;
  short attributed quotes and domain structure are fine.**

## 4. FAST — Family-centered Adolescent Sperm banking values clarification Tool (AYA-specific)

- **Authors:** Klosky, Nahata and colleagues (Nationwide Children's/St. Jude).
- **URLs read:**
  - Protocol: Stanek C et al., PLOS ONE 2022, "Study protocol for fertility preservation
    discussions and decisions..." https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0263886
  - Pilot RCT: Nahata L et al., Pediatric Blood & Cancer 2025,
    https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12187531/
- **What it is:** The only values-clarification tool built *for adolescents themselves* (males,
  sperm banking). 18 items, 4-point Likert (strongly disagree–strongly agree), domains from the
  Health Belief Model: perceived threat of infertility, perceived benefits of banking,
  perceived barriers. Adolescent and each caregiver complete it independently; an algorithm
  generates a concordance/discordance report; a facilitator then leads a brief guided family
  conversation (median ~10 min) over agreements, disagreements and "areas of uncertainty or
  knowledge gaps."
- **Verbatim example item (caregiver proxy form):** "My son is worried about having a biological
  child in the future because his child might get sick/cancer." Caregiver proxy items include an
  "unsure" response option.
- **Validation:** Pre/post implementation, banking attempts rose 30%→60% (JARG 2021,
  10.1007/s10815-021-02092-6); pilot RCT (FP-DAD): banking attempts 82% vs 67% (ns in pilot),
  decision-quality effect sizes medium-to-large, acceptability 88–100%.
- **Licensing:** Full item set not published openly; research instrument → **contact authors
  for items/permission. The design pattern (teen + parent answer separately, then compare) is
  freely adaptable.**

- **Publisher:** Oncofertility Consortium (now hosted at Michigan State University).
- **URLs:** https://www.savemyfertility.org/ (fetch on 2026-11-02 returned an empty page body —
  site may be degraded); https://oncofertility.msu.edu/online-resources/ (listing read via search).

## 6. Ottawa Personal Decision Guide (OPDG)

- **Publisher:** © 2025 Stacey, Lewis, O'Connor, Ottawa Hospital Research Institute &
  University of Ottawa (with Finderup, Kienlin, Persson Kylen).
- **URLs read:** https://decisionaid.ohri.ca/decguide.html and
  https://decisionaid.ohri.ca/docs/das/OPDG.pdf (2-page guide, read in full).
- **Validation:** The generic instrument of the Ottawa Decision Support Framework — the same
  framework BEFORE and CFM are built on; available in 15+ languages; step 4 is "Adapted from
  The SURE Test © 2008 O'Connor & Légaré" (validated decisional-conflict screen).

### The four steps, verbatim

1. **"Clarify your decision"** — "What decision do you face?" / "What are your reasons for
   making this decision?" / "When do you need to make a choice?" / "How far along are you with
   making a choice?" (options: "Not thought about it / Thinking about it / Close to choosing /
   Made a choice")
2. **"Explore your options"** — grid: "Options you can choose from (includes doing nothing or
   making no change)"; for each, "Reasons to Choose this Option — Benefits / Advantages / Pros"
   and "Reasons to Avoid this Option — Risks / Disadvantages / Cons", each rated "How much it
   matters to you: 0 not at all — 5 a great deal". Then: "Which option do you prefer?
   Option 1 / Option 2 / Option 3 / **Unsure**"
3. **"Explore views of others involved in the decision"** — "Who else is involved?" / "Which
   option does this person prefer?" / **"Are you feeling pressured by this person?"** (Yes/No) /
   "How can this person support you?"
4. **"Identify your needs and plan next steps based on them"** — screening questions: "Do you
   know the benefits and risks of each option?" / "Are you clear about which benefits and risks
   matter most to you?" / "Do you have enough support and advice to make a choice?" / "Do you
   feel sure about the best choice for you?" / "Are there other things making the decision
   difficult?" — each "No" maps to concrete next steps (find facts, review the stars, talk to
   people who have made the decision, find support). Includes a block: "If you feel PRESSURE
   from others to make a specific decision" → "Focus on the views of others who matter most",
   "Ask others to fill in this guide to see where you agree and disagree", "Take turns to listen
   to what the other person says matters most to them."

- **Licensing (from decisionaid.ohri.ca, read 2026-11-02):** copyrighted but free to use if you
  "cite the reference in any documents or publications", "do not charge for or profit from
  them", and "do not alter them except for prefilling them for a specific condition/decision as
  necessary". → **Best licensing position of any source: we may prefill the OPDG for the
  fertility-preservation decision with attribution, free of charge. Rewriting/altering its text
  beyond prefilling is not permitted; our own original prompts can sit alongside it.**

## 7. Reproductive Life Plan (CDC / HHS Office of Population Affairs)

- **Publisher:** CDC preconception-health guidance; disseminated via HHS OPA Title X and the
  Before, Between & Beyond Pregnancy toolkit (UNC).
- **URLs:** https://beforeandbeyond.org/toolkit/reproductive-life-plan-assessment/ (read
  2026-11-02; hhs.gov page returned 403). Montgomery County MD worksheet
  (reproductivelifeplan-worksheet.pdf) located via search as a CDC-derived public worksheet.
- **Questions, verbatim (CDC-derived, per Before, Between & Beyond):**
  - "Do you plan to have any (more) children at any time in your future?"
  - "How many children would you like to have?"
  - "How long would you like to wait until you [or your partner] becomes pregnant?"
  - "What family planning method do you plan to use until you [or your partner] are ready to
    become pregnant?"
  - "How sure are you that you will be able to use this method without any problems?"
  - "What family planning method will you use to avoid pregnancy?"
  - **"People's plans change. Is it possible you or your partner could ever decide to become
    pregnant?"** ← key: plans are revisitable; "not now / maybe someday" is an expected state.
- **Validation:** a public-health counseling framework (One Key Question lineage), not a
  psychometric instrument.
- **Licensing:** **US federal government work — public domain. Freely adaptable.**

## 8. Childbearing Motivations Scale (CMS) — Guedes et al.

- **Authors:** Guedes M, Pereira M, Pires R, Carvalho P, Canavarro MC (2013). "Childbearing
  Motivations Scale: Construction of a New Measure and its Preliminary Psychometric
  Properties." Journal of Child and Family Studies.
- **URL read:** https://emerge.ucsd.edu/r_1qfvucm3pqpk4uh/ (EMERGE measure repository entry;
  the Coimbra repository PDF would not download on 2026-11-02).
- **Structure:** 47 self-report items: positive childbearing motivations subscale (26 items)
  and negative subscale (21 items); response scale "1 (Not at all) to 5 (Completely)".
- **Example items, verbatim (via EMERGE and published abstracts):**
  - Positive: "Strengthening the bond with my partner"; "Realizing my maternal or paternal
    instinct"; "Creating my own family"; also reported: "Continuing my family name";
    "Listening to the demands of my biological clock"; "Giving a meaning to my life";
    "Being connected to a child through blood ties"; "Meeting my family's expectations".
  - Negative: "Facing the labor of childcare"; "Worrying about the future of a child in the
    current world"; "Losing autonomy as a couple".
- **Validation:** EMERGE rates psychometrics "HIGH" (formative research, internal reliability,
  construct validity); adults of both sexes, Portugal; Italian validation exists
  (PMC11855479).
- **Licensing:** academic instrument, items published in journal articles; no open license →
  **permission/citation needed to administer as a scale; the motivation *domains* (relational,
  identity/meaning, family continuity, social expectation vs childcare burden, autonomy loss,
  world-worry) are freely usable to organize our own prompts.**

### Related: Miller Childbearing Questionnaire (CBQ)

- Miller WB (1995), Transnational Family Research Institute; 49 items measuring positive and
  negative childbearing motivation (5 positive categories, 4 negative). Listed at
  https://emerge.ucsd.edu/r_3grcom7ameis7xj/ (located via search; not fetched). Sample items
  reported in the literature: "Holding and cuddling a baby"; "Devoting myself and much of my
  time to raising children and being a mother (father)". **Proprietary research instrument —
  licensing required; lower priority than CMS.**

## 9. Equipoise, "unsure" as a resting place, and timing pressure — what the literature says

- **IPDAS Chapter I** (Stalmeier P, Volk RJ, et al., 2012. "Balancing the presentation of
  information and options." IPDAS Collaboration background document; URL read:
  https://decisionaid.ohri.ca/IPDAS/ipdas-chapter-i.pdf). Verbatim definition: a decision aid
  is balanced to "the extent to which it presents—in content, in format, and in display—the
  available options and the positive and negative information about each of those options in a
  complete and neutral manner. The intention is to enable individuals to understand the options
  and their relevant information without influencing those individuals towards favouring or
  rejecting any particular option." If information is incomplete "people may inadvertently
  overvalue or devalue a treatment option."
  → Our flow must never grade answers, must give "no preservation / wait and see" equal
  billing, and must present downsides of preserving as carefully as downsides of not.
- **CFM paper, alpha-testing audit trail** (Frontiers Oncol 2022, Table 4): a request to add
  advice on "What to do if patients disagree with doctors' recommendations" was rejected as
  "Unethical, decision-aid is supposed to be neutral"; case studies were rejected because they
  "Would bias the decision aid." Developers treated neutrality as a hard constraint.
- **"Unsure" as a valid resting place:**
  - OPDG preference question offers "Unsure" explicitly; SURE-derived step 4 then routes
    unsure people to *needs* (facts, values clarity, support), not to a forced choice.
  - CFM option exercise midpoint is "Unsure"; booklet: "There is no 'right' or 'wrong'
    decision."
  - BEFORE: "I am not sure yet" is a checkbox outcome, and "You do not have to make your
    fertility decision after completing this exercise."
  - CDC RLP: "People's plans change..." — parenthood intent is allowed to be provisional.
- **Timing pressure is real and must be named, not weaponized:**
  - CFM paper (HCP quote): "You've got a short window of opportunity... It's a pressured
    decision-making process." Patients: "everything was just done so quickly, and you can't
    think at the time of what you need to do"; "I wish I'd read it beforehand so I would have
    actually known what to ask."
  - The decision that is time-boxed is *whether to preserve before treatment starts* — not
    *whether to ever be a parent*. BEFORE/CFM both separate these (preservation now vs
    parenthood options after treatment). Preservation is repeatedly framed as keeping options
    open, with the regret item running in both directions (BEFORE: regret "not keeping my
    options open" vs "I may regret going through this process if I am able to have a natural
    pregnancy after treatment").
  - OPDG step 3 asks directly about interpersonal pressure ("Are you feeling pressured by this
    person?") and gives de-escalation steps.
  - Evaluation literature (Speller et al. 2019, Curr Oncol — "An evaluation of oncofertility
    decision support resources," https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6366104/, located via
    search): most existing resources "do not directly include the option to forgo any fertility
    intervention" — a known equipoise gap our flow should not repeat.

# SYNTHESIS — recommended reflection flow for the "I'm not sure about children" path

Design rules derived above: never push a direction (IPDAS); "unsure" is always a selectable,
non-penalized answer (OPDG, CFM, BEFORE); separate the time-boxed decision (preserve now?) from
the open one (parent someday?); name the time pressure honestly (CFM); present wait-and-see /
no-preservation as a full option with its own pros (BEFORE); end on needs and next steps, not on
a verdict (OPDG step 4).

Eight steps. Every prompt traces to a named source; wording below is OURS (paraphrase) unless
quoted.

1. **Name the decision and its clock.** "What decision do you face? ... When do you need to
   make a choice?" (OPDG step 1, usable verbatim with citation). Clarify the two decisions:
   the one with a deadline (preserve before treatment starts) and the one without (whether to
   have children someday) — separation per BEFORE/CFM structure; timing honesty per CFM beta
   findings.
2. **Locate yourself, with "unsure" as a legal square.** "How far along are you with making a
   choice?" — "Not thought about it / Thinking about it / Close to choosing / Made a choice"
   (OPDG step 1); plus BEFORE's "I am not sure yet" as an explicit outcome.
3. **What parenthood could mean to me.** Reflection prompts organized by CMS motivation domains
   (relationship, identity/meaning, family continuity, social/family expectation; vs childcare
   burden, autonomy, worries about the world/health) — domains from Guedes CMS; health-worry
   angle from FAST's verbatim item (fear a child "might get sick/cancer"). Write original
   prompts; do not administer CMS/CBQ items without license.
4. **Plans are allowed to change.** CDC RLP, public domain, adapt freely: "Do you plan to have
   any (more) children at any time in your future?" and "People's plans change. Is it possible
   you... could ever decide to become pregnant?" — framed as: your answer today is a snapshot,
   not a contract.
5. **What preservation does and doesn't promise.** Honest-uncertainty framing per BEFORE
   ("I may still not be able to have a child after freezing my eggs"; periods returning ≠
   fertility) and CFM ("It is difficult to be certain how likely it is that..."). Preservation
   = buying future options at a price, not buying a baby.
6. **Biological and other paths to parenthood.** Mirror BEFORE's "Parenthood options after
   cancer treatment" domain: natural pregnancy, frozen eggs/embryos, IVF later, donor eggs or
   embryos, surrogacy, adoption/fostering (CFM also covers fostering + signposts). Point: "not
   preserving" does not equal "never a parent," and "preserving" is not the only door.
7. **Time, money, body realities + what matters most to me.** Values grid in OPDG step-2 form
   (pros/cons each rated 0–5 "how much it matters to you" — usable via prefilled OPDG with
   attribution), populated with the factor *domains* from BEFORE's exercise (cost, treatment
   delay "2 or more weeks", injections/clinic visits, emotional load, genetic relatedness,
   two-directional regret) and CFM's 1–4 "What matters most to you?" statements. End with
   preference question including "Unsure" (OPDG).
8. **Who to talk with, pressure check, and deciding-later-on-purpose.** OPDG step 3 ("Who else
   is involved?... Are you feeling pressured by this person?... How can this person support
   you?") + FAST's pattern of teen and parent answering separately then comparing; CFM's list
   of who to involve and support routes (counselling, peers, writing). Close with OPDG step 4's
   needs screen ("Do you feel sure about the best choice for you?" → if No, route to facts /
   values / support, never to a default option), and an explicit "decide later" card: what
   waiting keeps open, what it forecloses, and when the window actually closes — pros of
   wait-and-see from BEFORE's own item list ("no cost", "start my cancer treatment right away")
   against its named risk ("may regret not keeping my options open").
