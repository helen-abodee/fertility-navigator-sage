# Family building after treatment: source extraction (2026-11-17)

For the Paths to Parenthood build. All pages read via automated fetch today.

## 1. ACS: Fertility Assistance After Cancer (updated 2025-01-17)
https://cancer.org/cancer/managing-cancer/side-effects/fertility/fertility-assistance-after-cancer.html
- Using preserved material: embryo transfer (thawed embryos placed in the
  uterus), IUI (thawed sperm via catheter), IVF (frozen eggs or sperm combined
  in lab), IVF-ICSI (single sperm injected into an egg).
- Donor eggs: known/anonymous/frozen banks; screened for infections and
  genetic conditions; mental-health screening strongly recommended.
- Donor embryos: hormone prep for the uterus usually needed.
- Donor sperm: major US banks screen health, family history, STDs; anonymous
  or future-contact donors; typically used with IUI.
- Gestational carrier vs traditional surrogate distinction; "surrogacy laws
  vary"; legal counsel + mental-health evaluation emphasized.
- ADOPTION: agencies with cancer-survivor experience exist; ADA protects
  against adoption discrimination; costs roughly $6,000 (public/foster) to
  $20,000-$50,000 (private/international); process requires medical
  documentation and health-status letters from physicians.
- Living without children acknowledged as a real choice; counseling suggested
  when partners disagree.

## 2. ACS: Having a Baby After Cancer: Pregnancy (reviewed 2024-05-28)
https://www.cancer.org/cancer/managing-cancer/side-effects/fertility/pregnancy-after-cancer.html
- Waiting: "Some cancer care providers recommend people wait to get pregnant
  for at least 6 months after finishing chemotherapy"; others suggest 2 to 5
  years; depends on cancer type, stage, treatment, age.
- "Generally, pregnancy does not increase cancer recurrence likelihood";
  breast cancer nuance: pregnancy hormones might stimulate breast cancer
  cells, hence the 2-year advice there.
- Pelvic/uterine radiation can damage tissue and blood supply: higher chance
  of miscarriage, early birth, low birth weight.

## 3. POSITIVE trial (Partridge et al., NEJM 2023;388:1645-56, PMID 37133584)
- 516 women ≤42 with HR+ stage I-III breast cancer paused endocrine therapy
  (after 18-30 months) to attempt pregnancy. 74% had at least one pregnancy;
  63.8% at least one live birth; 365 babies. 3-year breast-cancer-event rate
  8.9% vs 9.2% in the external control cohort (did not exceed the safety
  threshold). Median follow-up 41 months; longer follow-up ongoing.

## 4. AFP: Post-Treatment Family Building
https://www.allianceforfertilitypreservation.org/get-informed/post-treatment-family-building/
- Donor egg process detail (donors 21-34, screening, retrieval, transfer);
  donor embryos (FDA screening guidance; counseling re telling the child;
  legal consultation re state parentage laws); donor sperm (180-day
  quarantine, washing, IUI timing); surrogacy/GC (evaluation, costs,
  compensation, legal authority questions).
- Post-treatment fertility testing: females FSH/estradiol day 3, AMH, antral
  follicle ultrasound; males semen analysis, sperm function, hormones,
  urinalysis for retrograde ejaculation, genetic testing.
- Page does NOT cover natural conception, adoption, fostering, childfree.
