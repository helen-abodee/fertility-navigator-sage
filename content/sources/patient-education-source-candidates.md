# Patient-education source candidates: fertility preservation decision tool (AYA cancer)

Survey of candidate sources for (a) procedure explainers with timelines, (b) semen-collection preparation, (c) survivor fertility assessment (AMH/FSH/LH, timing after chemo).

All claims below come from reading the cited pages. Date retrieved for everything: **2026-10-30**. Pages that could not be opened are marked as such; nothing is claimed about their content beyond what their own search listings show.

---

## 1. Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center (MSKCC) Patient & Caregiver Education

**Publisher:** Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center (NCI-designated comprehensive cancer center).

- **Sperm Banking** — https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/patient-education/sperm-banking (last updated 2024-02-22)
  Covers (b) in full: abstinence window ("It's best if you don't ejaculate during sex or masturbation for 2 to 5 days before the first collection"), collection in "a small private room" at the sperm bank, at-home collection if permitted, collection in hospital rooms if hospitalized, 3 specimens with 2–3 days between (daily with 1 day between if treatment starts soon), required infectious disease testing, no saliva/lubricant, yearly storage fee after the first year, approximate NYC cost ($800–$1,000 for three collections incl. first-year storage), alternatives if unable to collect. Companion pages exist for TESE, electroejaculation, and pre-pubertal testicular tissue extraction.
- **Fertility Preservation Before Cancer Treatment: Options for People Born with Ovaries and a Uterus** — https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/patient-education/fertility-preservation-females-starting-treatment (last updated 2019-10-23)
  Covers (a) for egg/embryo/ovarian tissue freezing in genuine step-by-step form: evaluation phase with transvaginal ultrasound and "blood tests to measure the levels of hormones related to fertility, including follicle stimulating hormone (FSH) and anti-mullerian hormone (AMH)"; stimulation phase ("give yourself hormone injections (shots) each day for about 10 days," monitoring "almost every other day for blood tests and ultrasounds"); retrieval ("the entire procedure takes about 20 minutes and most females are discharged within 1 hour"). Ovarian tissue freezing steps included (surgical removal, lab processing, freezing, re-implantation or IVM). Notes annual storage fee, thaw costs, and points to Livestrong Fertility for financial help. Caveat: 2019 date; some stats (e.g., "about 130 babies" from ovarian tissue) are stale.
- **Fertility Evaluation and Fertility Preservation After Cancer Treatment: Options for People Born with Ovaries and a Uterus** — https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/patient-education/fertility-evaluation-and-preservation-options-females-who-have-completed-treatment (last updated 2019-10-23)
  Covers (c) for females: names FSH and AMH blood tests and antral follicle count by transvaginal ultrasound. Timing guidance, verbatim: "We generally recommend that you wait at least 1 year after completing cancer treatment before having your fertility evaluated." Does not name LH or estradiol. No 100-day figure.
- **Building Your Family After Cancer Treatment: For People Born With Testicles** — https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/patient-education/building-family-born-with-testicles (last updated 2023-06-21)
  Covers (c) for males partially: "You can have a semen analysis done at least 1 year after your cancer treatment is complete"; "It can take up to 5 years to fully recover after cancer treatment." Does not cover FSH/LH/testosterone testing.

**Reading level:** Plain, defined terms, phonetic pronunciations; roughly 8th–10th grade. **Licensing:** "© 1996–2026 Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center, et al. All rights reserved." No open license; cite/link rather than reproduce, or request permission.

## 2. ASRM — ReproductiveFacts.org (American Society for Reproductive Medicine)

**Publisher:** ASRM, the US professional society for reproductive medicine — the strongest medical authority on the procedure mechanics themselves.

- **Fertility Preservation Journey (patient journey)** — https://www.reproductivefacts.org/patient-journeys/fertility-preservation/ (no visible update date)
  Covers (a) as an explicit "Fertility Preservation Process: Step by Step": consultation → method choice → treatment/collection (separate tracks for people with ovaries, sperm, or tissue) → storage and future use. Timelines given: "10-12 days" of hormone injections, "15-30 minutes" for egg retrieval, "two to three weeks" total for embryo freezing. Covers egg, sperm, embryo, ovarian tissue, and testicular tissue preservation.
- **Female Cancers, Cryopreservation, and Fertility (fact sheet)** — https://www.reproductivefacts.org/news-and-publications/fact-sheets-and-infographics/female-cancers-cryopreservation-and-fertility/ (revised 2023)
  Covers (a) at summary level: embryo and egg freezing each "may take two to three weeks"; ovarian tissue freezing described surgically. No day-by-day schedule, no storage fees. Downloadable PDF + infographic.
- **Cancer and its impact on sperm, cryopreservation, and fertility (fact sheet)** — https://www.reproductivefacts.org/news-and-publications/fact-sheets-and-infographics/cancer-and-its-impact-on-sperm/ (revised 2023)
  Covers (b) partially: collection in "a private room or asked to collect at home," may be asked for more than one sample, alternatives (special condoms, electroejaculation, surgical extraction), storage ("Sperm can be stored indefinitely. Sperm that have been frozen for over 20 years have been used to create pregnancies"). No abstinence window, no fees. Nothing on post-treatment testing.

**Reading level:** Accessible patient language; slightly more clinical than MSKCC. **Licensing:** "© 1996 - 2026 ASRM... All Rights Reserved." No open license visible.

## 3. NCI — cancer.gov fertility pages

**Publisher:** National Cancer Institute (NIH). Note the pages have been retitled since our candidate list was drafted.

- **Female Fertility and Cancer Treatment** — https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/side-effects/fertility-women (updated 2025-05-14)
- **Male Fertility and Cancer Treatment** — https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/side-effects/fertility-men (updated 2025-05-14)

Coverage: (a)/(b) at overview level only. Egg/embryo freezing described in one or two sentences each ("hormone level tests and an ultrasound" then fertility drugs, harvest, fertilize, freeze); ovarian tissue freezing one sentence; sperm "collected during masturbation at home or in a private room," electroejaculation noted; testicular tissue flagged as for pre-pubertal boys/clinical trials. No timelines, no abstinence window, no sedation detail, no fees, and nothing on (c) (no AMH/FSH/LH, no post-chemo timing).

**Reading level:** Plain language with linked glossary definitions; very readable. **Licensing:** Best of the lot — "If you would like to reproduce some or all of this content, see Reuse of NCI Information"; most NCI-authored text is federal public domain, which matters if we want to adapt wording rather than just cite.

## 4. NCCN Guidelines for Patients: Adolescent and Young Adult Cancer (2023)

**Publisher:** National Comprehensive Cancer Network. PDF: https://www.nccn.org/patients/guidelines/content/PDF/aya-patient.pdf (2023 edition, 70 pp; fertility chapter pp. 9–16).

Coverage: the best AYA-specific *framing* source — fertility risk by treatment type, the oncofertility team roster, out-of-pocket cost warnings, decision questions ("How do you feel about having children in the future?"), and a one-page options table (Guide 1) spanning egg freezing, embryo freezing/IVF, ovarian transposition, ovarian tissue freezing, sperm banking, testicular sperm extraction, testicular tissue freezing. Timelines are coarse: egg/embryo freezing "usually takes about two and half weeks"; "Some types of fertility preservation take a couple of days." Sperm banking: "Sperm banking kits can be ordered online or you can make an appointment at a local urology office" — no abstinence window. Nothing on (c) labs; "Fertility after treatment" section covers donor/adoption/surrogacy options only.

**Reading level:** Written for AYA patients; clear and warm. **Licensing:** NCCN copyright, all rights reserved; NCCN is protective of its content — cite and link only.

## 5. Cleveland Clinic

**Publisher:** Cleveland Clinic (my.clevelandclinic.org Health Library).

- **Fertility Preservation: Purpose, Procedure Options & Benefits** — https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/17000-fertility-preservation (last reviewed 2026-02-10 — the freshest review date found in this survey)
  Covers all five procedures (egg, embryo, ovarian tissue, sperm, testicular tissue). Timelines: "You'll inject yourself with hormones daily for about 10 days"; egg freezing "can take up to three weeks"; sperm freezing "takes a few days"; laparoscopy discharge "about two hours after." No AMH/FSH/LH, no fees.
- **Embryo Freezing (Cryopreservation)** — https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/15464-embryo-freezing-cryopreservation (last reviewed 2022-02-17)
  Freezing/thawing science and consent items ("How long they'll be stored (often 10 years)"), but no stimulation/retrieval walkthrough, no sedation text, no fees.
- **Sperm Banking** — https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/17493-sperm-banking — **could not be opened** (403/redirect loop for automated clients on 2026-10-30). Search listings indicate it covers collection, freezing, storage, annual storage fee, and at-home kits, but this is unverified; needs a manual check in a browser before use.
- **Cancer and Fertility: Planning for Parenthood After a Cancer Diagnosis (brochure PDF)** — https://my.clevelandclinic.org/-/scassets/files/org/cancer/patient-education/cnr-938-cancer-fertility.pdf (no date shown; calls ovarian tissue freezing "experimental," so it predates ASRM's 2019 de-labeling — treat as dated)
  Nice cancer-specific framing (semen analysis after treatment, "damage may take a year or so to repair" for sperm genetic material, safety-of-pregnancy FAQ), but procedures are summary-level ("Both embryo freezing and egg freezing take from two to six weeks") and the hormone tests are unnamed ("A reproductive specialist can estimate the number of eggs that remain using hormone tests and ultrasound").

**Reading level:** Consumer-friendly, terms hyperlinked. **Licensing:** "© 2026 Cleveland Clinic. All Rights Reserved."

## 8. Livestrong Fertility

**Publisher:** Livestrong Foundation.

- **Livestrong Fertility program page** — https://livestrong.org/how-we-help/livestrong-fertility/ — primarily a financial-assistance/discount program (≥25% off services, partner clinics, brochures in English/Spanish). Not a procedure explainer. Useful to link from any "paying for it" section.
- **Male Fertility Preservation (resource article)** — https://livestrong.org/resources/male-fertility-preservation/ (last updated 4.16.24)
  Sperm banking basics: "Masturbation is the preferred method"; samples "can be kept for at least 20 to 30 years"; "Most health insurance plans do not cover the cost of storing frozen semen." No abstinence window, no sample counts, no fee amounts. Post-treatment: "semen analysis will usually improve within one to three years after finishing cancer treatment. However, some men improve many years later." Testicular tissue freezing called experimental with no live births — consistent with the human evidence but phrased absolutely; verify before quoting.

**Reading level:** Accessible adult. **Licensing:** © 2015–2026 Livestrong Foundation; no open license.

## 9. American Cancer Society

- **Preserving Fertility in Children and Teens with Cancer** — https://www.cancer.org/cancer/managing-cancer/side-effects/fertility/preserving-fertility-in-children-and-teens-with-cancer.html (last revised 2025-01-13)

ACS also has companion adult male/female fertility pages (URLs have shifted; one candidate URL 404'd — verify current paths before citing).

**Reading level:** High-school/adult. **Licensing:** "American Cancer Society medical information is copyrighted material. For reprint requests, please see our Content Usage Policy."

## 11. Children's Oncology Group Health Links (survivorshipguidelines.org)

**Publisher:** Children's Oncology Group — the authoritative survivorship follow-up body for childhood/AYA cancer; Health Links are the patient-facing companions to the COG Long-Term Follow-Up Guidelines (v6.0, October 2023).

- **Testicular Reproductive Health, v6.0 (Oct 2023)** — https://childrensoncologygroup.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/30_Testicular_Reproductive_Health_v6-secured.pdf
  Covers (c) male thoroughly: yearly check-up with blood tests for "AM testosterone, LH, FSH, and inhibin"; "The best way to assess the ability to make sperm is a semen analysis... The specimen is produced after several days of abstinence. If the patient is unable to produce a semen specimen or prefers not to, an FSH and inhibin level may provide some insight... A high FSH or low inhibin suggest impaired ability to make sperm"; azoospermia "on more than one sample is an indicator of infertility"; recovery "usually happens in the first few years after completion of cancer treatment." No 100-day figure.
- **Ovarian Reproductive Health, v6.0 (Oct 2023)** — https://childrensoncologygroup.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/17_Ovarian_Reproductive_Health_v6-secured.pdf
  Covers (c) female: yearly check-up; "Blood may be tested for hormone levels (FSH, LH, and estradiol) if a problem is suspected"; regular periods + normal FSH/LH/estradiol → likely fertile. Notably does NOT mention AMH. No 100-day figure.

**Reading level:** Patient-friendly but denser than MSKCC/St. Jude. **Licensing:** "Copyright 2023 © Children's Oncology Group. All rights reserved worldwide."

# THE "100 DAYS AFTER CHEMO" VERDICT

**The figure does NOT appear in any reputable patient-education source surveyed.** The only hit anywhere is a commercial clinic page, The Turek Clinic (https://www.theturekclinic.com/male-fertility-preservation/, retrieved 2026-10-30), which states verbatim: *"This rate of chromosomal damage returns to baseline levels (pre-treatment) 100 days after chemotherapy ends."* — with no citation, no date, and note that it describes sperm chromosomal damage resolving, **not** when to test fertility. It was checked for and absent in: NCI (both pages), ASRM (both fact sheets + journey), NCCN AYA patient guide, MSKCC (all four pages), Cleveland Clinic (page + brochure), ACS, Livestrong, Together by St. Jude, and both COG Health Links. The reputable, citable timing guidance that exists instead: MSKCC — "wait at least 1 year after completing cancer treatment before having your fertility evaluated" (female) and semen analysis "at least 1 year after your cancer treatment is complete" (male); Livestrong — semen analysis "will usually improve within one to three years." **Recommendation: drop the 100-day figure and source timing to MSKCC's one-year guidance.**

---

# RANKED RECOMMENDATION

**(a) Procedure explainers with timelines**
1. **MSKCC "Fertility Preservation Before Cancer Treatment" (ovaries/uterus)** — the only source with the full chain we need: baseline labs (FSH, AMH, ultrasound) → ~10 days of self-injections → every-other-day monitoring → ~20-minute retrieval with 1-hour discharge → freezing/storage/fees, plus ovarian tissue steps. Pair with **ASRM's Fertility Preservation Journey** for authoritative cross-confirmation of the 10–12-day stimulation and 15–30-minute retrieval figures and for sperm/testicular tracks. (Backup: Cleveland Clinic Fertility Preservation, reviewed 2026-02-10 — freshest date, "about 10 days" corroboration.)

**(b) Semen-collection preparation guide**
1. **MSKCC "Sperm Banking"** — it alone has everything the brief asks for: 2–5-day abstinence window, private-room and in-hospital collection, 3 samples at 2–3-day spacing (compressed schedule if treatment is imminent), infectious-disease screening, lubricant/saliva rules, storage fees. 2. **ACS "Preserving Fertility in Children and Teens with Cancer"** as the AYA-sensitive overlay (teen embarrassment, national cost ranges $500–$1,000 + $200–$500/yr).

**(c) Survivor fertility assessment**
1. **COG Health Links (Testicular + Ovarian Reproductive Health, v6.0 2023)** — the authoritative survivorship source naming the exact labs per sex (male: AM testosterone, LH, FSH, inhibin, semen analysis after several days of abstinence; female: FSH, LH, estradiol) and tied to the COG LTFU Guidelines. 2. **MSKCC "Fertility Evaluation After Cancer Treatment"** for AMH + antral follicle count (which COG's female sheet omits) and the citable "wait at least 1 year" timing.

**Rationale.** MSKCC emerges as the backbone for (a) and (b) because it is the only reputable publisher whose pages operate at the altitude this tool needs — actual day counts, abstinence windows, sample schedules, fees — while everything else (NCI, ASRM fact sheets, NCCN, ACS, St. Jude) stays one level up at "what the options are." ASRM earns the co-slot on (a) as the professional society of record whose step-by-step journey independently confirms MSKCC's timelines, insulating us against MSKCC's 2019 review dates on the female pages. For (c), COG + MSKCC together cover every lab in the brief (AMH, FSH, LH) with a defensible answer to "when": the 100-day figure should be dropped as unsourceable at patient-education grade, replaced by MSKCC's one-year guidance. NCI remains worth citing wherever we want to adapt wording outright, because it is the only source with public-domain-friendly reuse terms; NCCN AYA is the best model for decision framing and teen voice even though its procedure detail is coarse. SaveMyFertility and Mayo could not be read (bot walls) and should not be cited until someone verifies them manually.
