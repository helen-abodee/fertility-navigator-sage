# Fertility health source notes

Patient-level facts about everyday factors affecting egg and sperm health, for the fertility preservation tool (AYA cancer patients and survivors). Every fact below is supported by a verbatim quote from the cited page. Facts are framed as risk and mechanism information, never as "do X to boost fertility." Retrieved date for all entries: 2026-11-02.

Sources used:

- ASRM ReproductiveFacts.org patient fact sheets: Smoking and Infertility (Revised 2023), Optimizing Natural Fertility (Revised 2023), Optimizing Male Fertility (Revised 2023), Testosterone Use and Male Infertility (Revised 2021), Does My Age Affect My Fertility? (Revised 2023)
- ASRM Practice Committee opinion: Tobacco or Marijuana Use and Infertility (2024)
- Cleveland Clinic: Can Drugs Lower Your Sperm Count? (Nov 10, 2022, reviewed by urologist Sarah Vij, MD), Oligospermia (updated 02/27/2025), Male Infertility (updated 01/25/2024), Male Reproductive System (updated 05/08/2023)

## 1. Cannabis and sperm

### Fact 1.1: THC can decrease sperm production
- Plain language: The THC in cannabis can lower how much sperm the body makes, and may also slow sperm movement.
- Mechanism: THC interferes with testosterone production, the hormone sperm production depends on.
- Applies to: sperm.
- Verbatim support: "THC can decrease sperm production and weakens sexual drive by interfering with testosterone production." "It may also limit movement of sperm."
- Source: Cleveland Clinic, "Can Drugs Lower Your Sperm Count?"
- URL: https://health.clevelandclinic.org/can-drugs-lower-sperm-count
- Page date: November 10, 2022 (reviewed by Sarah Vij, MD). Retrieved 2026-11-02.

### Fact 1.2: Studies of men in the general population link marijuana use to worse semen quality
- Plain language: In studies of men from the general population, marijuana use has been associated with lower semen quality on nearly every measure.
- Mechanism: not stated in the quoted passage (the committee opinion discusses cannabinoid receptors in the reproductive tract elsewhere; do not overstate).
- Applies to: sperm.
- Verbatim support: "Studies comprising men from the general population have found positive associations between marijuana use and diminished semen quality across all parameters aside from semen volume." Also: marijuana use "was associated with an increased risk of abnormal sperm morphology across three studies conducted in the UK, Pacific Northwest, and Jamaica."
- EVIDENCE CAVEAT (state honestly): the same document notes that studies of men at infertility clinics "have found either no appreciable associations or inconsistent associations between marijuana use and examined semen parameters," and "there is no clear association between male marijuana use and fecundability in the few existing studies."
- Source: ASRM Practice Committee, "Tobacco or marijuana use and infertility: a committee opinion."
- URL: https://www.asrm.org/practice-guidance/practice-committee-documents/tobacco-or-marijuana-use/
- Page date: 2024. Retrieved 2026-11-02.

### Fact 1.3: ASRM patient guidance calls the evidence mixed but advises limiting use
- Plain language: Evidence on whether marijuana lowers male fertility is mixed; ASRM's patient sheet says limited or no use is the safest option.
- Applies to: sperm.
- Verbatim support: "There is mixed evidence about whether marijuana decreases male fertility but limited or no use is probably the safest option for optimizing chances of getting pregnant."
- Source: ASRM ReproductiveFacts.org, "Optimizing Male Fertility" fact sheet.
- URL: https://www.reproductivefacts.org/news-and-publications/fact-sheets-and-infographics/optimizing-male-fertility-factsheet/
- Page date: Revised 2023. Retrieved 2026-11-02.

### Fact 1.4: Recreational drugs including marijuana are associated with reduced fertility (both sexes)
- Plain language: Marijuana and other recreational drug use has been associated with reduced fertility.
- Applies to: both.
- Verbatim support: "Smoking, heavy alcohol consumption (> 2 drinks per day), heavy caffeine consumption, and the use of recreational drugs such as marijuana have all been associated with reduced fertility."
- Source: ASRM ReproductiveFacts.org, "Optimizing Natural Fertility" fact sheet.
- URL: https://www.reproductivefacts.org/news-and-publications/fact-sheets-and-infographics/optimizing-natural-fertility/
- Page date: Revised 2023. Retrieved 2026-11-02.

Shipping note for 1.x: "Cannabis can lower sperm count" is supportable, citing Cleveland Clinic (1.1) for the direct statement and mechanism, with the ASRM committee opinion (1.2) as the stronger evidentiary citation. Pair it with the mixed-evidence caveat from 1.2/1.3 for honesty.

## 2. Tobacco, smoking, and vaping

### Fact 2.1: Smoking lowers sperm count, motility, and normal shape
- Plain language: Men who smoke can have fewer sperm, sperm that move less well, and more abnormally shaped sperm.
- Mechanism (from the 2024 committee opinion): tobacco smoke constituents can bind directly to sperm DNA; "Cigarette smoke contains toxic oxygen-reactive species that help produce adducts and are mutagenic in their own right."
- Applies to: sperm.
- Verbatim support: "Male smokers can suffer decreased sperm quality with lower counts (numbers of sperm) and motility (sperm's ability to move) and increased numbers of abnormally-shaped sperm." "Smoking might also decrease the sperm's ability to fertilize eggs."
- Source: ASRM ReproductiveFacts.org, "Smoking and Infertility" fact sheet.
- URL: https://www.reproductivefacts.org/news-and-publications/fact-sheets-and-infographics/smoking-and-infertility/
- Page date: Revised 2023. Retrieved 2026-11-02.

### Fact 2.2: Chemicals in cigarette smoke speed up egg loss, and lost eggs do not come back
- Plain language: Nicotine, cyanide, and carbon monoxide in cigarette smoke make eggs die off faster. Once eggs die, the body cannot replace them. Menopause comes 1 to 4 years earlier in smokers.
- Mechanism: named chemicals accelerate the loss rate of eggs; "Chemicals in cigarette smoke appear to accelerate follicular depletion and the loss of reproductive function" (committee opinion wording).
- Applies to: eggs.
- Verbatim support: "Chemicals (such as nicotine, cyanide, and carbon monoxide) in cigarette smoke speed up the loss rate of eggs." "once eggs die off, they cannot regenerate or be replaced." "menopause occurs 1 to 4 years earlier in women who smoke compared with non-smokers."
- Source: ASRM ReproductiveFacts.org, "Smoking and Infertility" fact sheet (URL and date as in 2.1); mechanism quote from ASRM committee opinion 2024 (URL as in 1.2).
- Retrieved 2026-11-02.

### Fact 2.3: Egg loss from smoking is permanent even after quitting; other smoking harms lessen over time
- Plain language: Stopping smoking does not bring lost eggs back, but pregnancy complication rates fall the longer someone has not smoked.
- Applies to: eggs (permanence); both (complication rates).
- Verbatim support: "Quitting smoking can improve fertility, though the decrease of the egg supply cannot be reversed." "The rate of pregnancy complications due to smoking decreases the longer a person has not smoked."
- Source: ASRM "Smoking and Infertility" fact sheet (URL and date as in 2.1). Retrieved 2026-11-02.
- Framing note: restate neutrally (further harm stops; already lost eggs are not restored). The "can improve fertility" phrasing is the source's, not ours.

### Fact 2.4: Smokers' infertility rates are about twice those of nonsmokers; secondhand smoke also carries risk
- Applies to: both.
- Verbatim support: infertility rates in male and female smokers are "about twice the rate of infertility found in nonsmokers" (fact sheet summary wording); "Women exposed to secondhand smoke can suffer all the above health risks even if they are not smoking themselves."
- Source: ASRM "Smoking and Infertility" fact sheet (URL and date as in 2.1). Retrieved 2026-11-02.

### Fact 2.5: Smoking is measurably linked to lower ovarian reserve markers
- Plain language: In studies, a blood marker of remaining egg supply (AMH) was 44% lower in current smokers than in never-smokers.
- Applies to: eggs.
- Verbatim support: "AMH levels were 44% lower in current smokers compared with never-smokers."
- Source: ASRM committee opinion 2024 (URL as in 1.2). Retrieved 2026-11-02.

### Fact 2.6: Vaping is not an established safe alternative; human evidence is limited
- Plain language: Human data on vaping and fertility are scarce. One human study found vape users had lower sperm concentration and total sperm count; animal data suggest reproductive harm.
- Applies to: both (evidence gap); sperm (the single human study).
- Verbatim support: "There is a dearth of evidence regarding the effect of ENDS on reproductive health with regard to conception, ovarian reserve depletion, sperm parameters, or ART outcomes in humans." "men who reported the use of ENDS had lower sperm concentration and total sperm count compared with nonusers." "early data from animal studies involving ENDS suggest overall harm to reproductive health."
- Source: ASRM committee opinion 2024 (URL as in 1.2). Retrieved 2026-11-02.
- Note evidence level clearly if shipped: one epidemiological human study.

## 3. Alcohol

### Fact 3.1: Heavy alcohol use is associated with reduced fertility
- Plain language: Heavy drinking (more than 2 drinks per day) has been associated with reduced fertility.
- Applies to: both (ASRM states it for people trying to conceive generally).
- Verbatim support: "Smoking, heavy alcohol consumption (> 2 drinks per day), heavy caffeine consumption, and the use of recreational drugs such as marijuana have all been associated with reduced fertility."
- Source: ASRM "Optimizing Natural Fertility" fact sheet (URL as in 1.4, Revised 2023). Retrieved 2026-11-02.

### Fact 3.2: Heavy drinking can lower testosterone, which can harm sperm production
- Plain language: Heavy alcohol use (defined as 10 to 14 or more drinks per week) can lower testosterone levels, and that can harm sperm production.
- Mechanism: lowered testosterone.
- Applies to: sperm.
- Verbatim support: "Heavy alcohol consumption — defined as 10 to 14 drinks or more per week — is another story." "All of this can lower your testosterone levels, which can harm sperm production."
- Source: Cleveland Clinic, "Can Drugs Lower Your Sperm Count?" (URL and date as in 1.1). Retrieved 2026-11-02.
- Eggs: no egg-specific alcohol mechanism was stated by these sources; do not invent one.

## 4. Heat exposure and sperm

### Fact 4.1: Sperm development needs the testes slightly cooler than body temperature
- Plain language: The testicles have to stay a little cooler than the rest of the body to make sperm normally. The scrotum works as a climate-control system, moving the testicles closer to the body for warmth and away from it to cool them.
- Mechanism: this IS the mechanism fact. Normal sperm development requires 97 to 99 degrees F (36 to 37 C), below core body temperature.
- Applies to: sperm.
- Verbatim support: "For normal sperm development, your testes must be at a temperature that's slightly cooler than body temperature (between 97 and 99 degrees Fahrenheit or 36 and 37 degrees Celsius)." "Your scrotum protects your testicles and provides a sort of 'climate-control system.'"
- Source: Cleveland Clinic, "Male Reproductive System."
- URL: https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/9117-male-reproductive-system
- Page date: last updated 05/08/2023. Retrieved 2026-11-02.

### Fact 4.2: Hot tubs and laptops on the lap raise scrotal temperature
- Plain language: ASRM advises avoiding things that raise scrotal temperature, naming hot tubs and laptops used on the lap.
- Applies to: sperm.
- Verbatim support: "Avoiding situations that absolutely can raise scrotal temperature (like hot tubs or using laptops on your lap) is recommended to preserve healthy sperm."
- Source: ASRM "Optimizing Male Fertility" fact sheet (URL as in 1.3, Revised 2023). Retrieved 2026-11-02.

### Fact 4.3: Long time in very hot environments is a cause of low sperm count; saunas and hot tubs named
- Applies to: sperm.
- Verbatim support: "Spending a long time in very hot environments, like a hot tub" (listed cause of low sperm count); "Spend a lot of time around heat that raises the temperature of your testicles, including saunas or hot tubs" (male infertility risk factor).
- Sources: Cleveland Clinic, "Oligospermia (Low Sperm Count)," https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/22477-oligospermia-low-sperm-count, updated 02/27/2025; Cleveland Clinic, "Male Infertility," https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/17201-male-infertility, updated 01/25/2024. Retrieved 2026-11-02.

### Fact 4.4: Illness (including fever) can affect sperm for months afterward
- Plain language: Because a sperm production cycle is long, being sick or on certain medications in the past three months can impair the sperm present today.
- Applies to: sperm.
- Verbatim support: "Because sperm take over 70 days (about 2 and a half months) to develop and mature, illness or medications within three months of producing the sample for testing can impair sperm ejaculated on the day of examination."
- Source: ASRM "Optimizing Male Fertility" fact sheet (URL as in 1.3, Revised 2023). Retrieved 2026-11-02.
- Note: ASRM says "illness," not specifically "fever"; keep the wording at "illness" or "being sick, such as with a fever" only if framed as our explanation, not the source's claim.

## 5. Testosterone supplements and anabolic steroids

### Fact 5.1: Taking testosterone usually drives sperm counts very low or to zero
- Plain language: Testosterone treatment usually leads to low sperm counts or no sperm at all in semen. This is clinically important and often unknown.
- Mechanism: "When you take testosterone, the hormonal signals that tell your testicles to make testosterone and to make sperm are blocked." (Cleveland Clinic)
- Applies to: sperm.
- Verbatim support: "testosterone treatment usually leads to either low sperm counts, or no sperm seen whatsoever in the semen." (ASRM)
- Sources: ASRM ReproductiveFacts.org, "Testosterone Use and Male Infertility," https://www.reproductivefacts.org/news-and-publications/fact-sheets-and-infographics/testosterone-use-and-male-infertility/, Revised 2021; Cleveland Clinic "Can Drugs Lower Your Sperm Count?" (URL as in 1.1). Retrieved 2026-11-02.

### Fact 5.2: The suppression is usually reversible after stopping, on a months timescale
- Plain language: ASRM: after stopping testosterone, sperm should return to the semen within about 3 months, with no permanent damage seen. Cleveland Clinic: for most people the effect is reversible but can take 6 to 12 months or more.
- Applies to: sperm.
- Verbatim support: "Once the testosterone is stopped, the sperm should return to the semen within about 3 months, and no permanent damage is seen." (ASRM) "In most people, this effect is reversible, though it can take six to 12 months or more for sperm production to return to normal." (Cleveland Clinic)
- Sources: as in 5.1. Retrieved 2026-11-02.
- Note the two timeframes differ; if shipped, say "months, roughly 3 to 12."

### Fact 5.3: Anabolic steroids impair sperm production the same way
- Mechanism: "Anabolic steroids harm male fertility by interfering with the hormone signals that are needed to produce sperm."
- Applies to: sperm.
- Verbatim support: quote above, plus "Many people recover sperm production three to 12 months after stopping anabolic steroids." (Cleveland Clinic) And from ASRM: "Recreational drugs, including anabolic steroids and testosterone, are associated with impaired sperm function and production and should not be used."
- Sources: Cleveland Clinic "Can Drugs Lower Your Sperm Count?" (URL as in 1.1); ASRM "Optimizing Male Fertility" (URL as in 1.3). Retrieved 2026-11-02.

## 6. Opioids and other medications

### Fact 6.1: Long-term opioid use can lower testosterone and reduce sperm quantity and quality
- Mechanism: opioids disrupt the hormonal signals that control testosterone production.
- Applies to: sperm.
- Verbatim support: "Long-term opioid use can disrupt the signals that control testosterone production, which can cause low testosterone and decrease both the quantity and quality of your sperm."
- Source: Cleveland Clinic, "Can Drugs Lower Your Sperm Count?" (URL as in 1.1). Retrieved 2026-11-02.
- Related: Cleveland Clinic's oligospermia page lists methadone among medications affecting sperm count (URL as in 4.3).

## 7. Sperm regeneration timescale

### Fact 7.1: A sperm production cycle takes over 70 days, about 2.5 months
- Plain language: Sperm take over 70 days (about 2 and a half months) to develop and mature. Any change, good or bad, takes roughly three months to show up in semen.
- Applies to: sperm.
- Verbatim support: "Because sperm take over 70 days (about 2 and a half months) to develop and mature, illness or medications within three months of producing the sample for testing can impair sperm ejaculated on the day of examination." "Remember that any lifestyle changes can take almost three months to show up as an improvement in the semen analysis."
- Source: ASRM "Optimizing Male Fertility" fact sheet (URL as in 1.3, Revised 2023). Retrieved 2026-11-02.

## 8. Eggs: modifiable vs not

### Fact 8.1: A woman is born with all her eggs; nothing grows new ones
- Plain language: Unlike sperm, eggs are not made continuously. A woman is born with all the eggs she will ever have, and there are no treatments to grow new eggs or preserve the quality of the remaining ones. Number and quality decline naturally with age.
- Applies to: eggs.
- Verbatim support: "a woman is born with all her eggs"; "there are no methods/ treatments to grow more or new eggs or preserve the quality of those remaining eggs."; eggs "decrease naturally and progressively from the time a woman is born."
- Source: ASRM ReproductiveFacts.org, "Does My Age Affect My Fertility?"
- URL: https://www.reproductivefacts.org/news-and-publications/fact-sheets-and-infographics/does-my-age-affect-my-fertility/
- Page date: Revised 2023. Retrieved 2026-11-02.

### Fact 8.2: Smoking is the clearly sourced modifiable factor for eggs
- Plain language: Of everyday factors, smoking is the one top-tier sources state plainly harms eggs (see 2.2, 2.3, 2.5). The age fact sheet adds, more softly: "Studies suggest that smoking, diet, and stress may have an impact on the quality of eggs/sperm and may accelerate menopause."
- Applies to: eggs (and sperm in that quote).
- Source: ASRM "Does My Age Affect My Fertility?" (URL as in 8.1) plus the smoking sources in section 2. Retrieved 2026-11-02.
- Do NOT ship diet or supplement claims for eggs; see 9.2.

## 9. Debunks (things sources say do NOT help)

### Fact 9.1: Position, orgasm, and lying down afterward do not change conception odds
- Verbatim support: "There is no evidence that sexual position, orgasm, or prolonged rest after intercourse increases the chance of conception."
- Source: ASRM "Optimizing Natural Fertility" (URL as in 1.4, Revised 2023). Retrieved 2026-11-02.

### Fact 9.2: No scientific evidence supports supplements for fertility
- Verbatim support: "Regarding all supplements, there is no scientific evidence currently promoting their use in enhancing fertility."
- Applies to: both.
- Source: ASRM "Optimizing Natural Fertility" (URL as in 1.4, Revised 2023). Retrieved 2026-11-02.

### Fact 9.3: Normal diet variation does not affect fertility in normal-weight people with regular periods
- Verbatim support: "there is no evidence that normal diet variations affect individuals who are of normal weight (body mass index 19-25) and having regular periods."
- Applies to: eggs/female fertility as stated.
- Source: ASRM "Optimizing Natural Fertility" (URL as in 1.4, Revised 2023). Retrieved 2026-11-02.
- Audience note: frame purely as information; no weight prescriptions for this audience.

### Fact 9.4: Underwear choice: evidence mixed, pick what is comfortable
- Verbatim support: "Other studies have found no link so underwear choice should still be based on what is most comfortable."
- Applies to: sperm.
- Source: ASRM "Optimizing Male Fertility" (URL as in 1.3, Revised 2023). Retrieved 2026-11-02.

### Fact 9.5 (bonus, actionable): Some common lubricants reduce sperm motility; others do not
- Verbatim support: "Some lubricants (Astroglide®, KY® Jelly, KY® TouchTM, saliva and olive oil) used during intercourse may decrease sperm motility (movement) or survival." "Others (Pre-Seed®, mineral oil or canola oil) have no such effect and can be used as needed."
- Applies to: sperm.
- Source: ASRM "Optimizing Natural Fertility" (URL as in 1.4, Revised 2023). Retrieved 2026-11-02.
