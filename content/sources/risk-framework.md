### 1a. Fertility Risks for Women

Category definitions (verbatim):
- **High Risk:** ">70% of women develop amenorrhea post-treatment. Any treatments containing high doses of alkylating agents and/or radiation to the abdomen, pelvis or hypothalamic axis present the highest level of risk for gonadal impact and immediate amenorrhea."
- **Intermediate Risk:** "~30-70% of women develop amenorrhea post-treatment. ... For Bevacizumab, risk of amenorrhea is intermediate, yet the outcome of fertility is unknown."
- **Lower Risk:** "<30% of women develop amenorrhea post-treatment. These treatments are unlikely to cause immediate amenorrhea at standard dosage; however, patients should be counseled that they may be at risk for early menopause."
- **Very Low/No Risk:** "Negligible to no effects on menses."
- **Unknown:** "Patients should be counseled regarding the lack of conclusive data about the reproductive effects of these drugs."

### 1b. Fertility Risks for Men

Category definitions (verbatim):
- **High Risk:** "Prolonged/permanent azoospermia is common after treatment. Any treatments containing high doses of alkylating agents and/or radiation to the testes, pelvis or hypothalamic axis present the highest level of risk for gonadal impact."
- **Intermediate Risk:** "Prolonged/permanent azoospermia is not common after treatment, but can occur. Lower levels of alkylating agents and/or radiation to the testes, pelvis or hylpthalamic [sic] axis reduce but do not eliminate the risk of sterility."
- **Lower Risk:** "Treatments typically cause only temporary damage to sperm production. While treatment itself is unlikely to cause sterility, patients may want to consider fertility preservation due to potential for relapse and/or additional treatment."
- **Very Low/No Risk:** (no separate considerations text in the men's chart; sidebar band only)

### 1c. Current livestrong.org pages (what is live today)

- https://livestrong.org/resources/female-fertility-preservation/ — carries only a simplified drug table (verbatim): **High Risk:** "Cyclophosphamide (Cytoxan), Cholarambucil [sic] (Leukeran), Melphalan (Alkeran, Medphalan, Merphalan, Sarcolysin), Busulfan (Myleran), Nitrogen Mustard (Mustargen), Procarbazine (Natulan, Matulane)"; **Intermediate Risk:** "Cisplatin (Platinol, Platinol-AQ), Adriamycin (Doxorubicin), Bleomycin (Blenoxane, Bleomycin), Actinomycin D (Dactinomycin, Cosmegen)"; **Low Risk:** "Methotrexate (Rheumatrex, Folex PFS), 5-Fluorouracil (Adrucil)". Plus: surgery "that removes the uterus, fallopian tubes or ovaries" and "radiation treatment directed to cancer in the abdomen" as causes of infertility.
- https://livestrong.org/resources/cancer-and-fertility-risks-for-men/ — narrative only, **no categories**: "The alkylating chemotherapy group does the most damage to fertility"; mild testicular radiation dose "may decrease but could recover over the next one–four years"; high dose "may permanently damage" sperm production; "Total body irradiation used before some bone marrow transplants often causes permanent infertility"; surgery effects (prostate/bladder removal; ejaculatory dysfunction after testicular or colon cancer surgery).

**Caveat for the live female page's intermediate band:** note bleomycin/actinomycin D sit in "Intermediate" there, but in the full archived charts ABVD (which contains bleomycin) is Lower risk. The simplified live table is drug-level, the archived charts are regimen-level; do not mix them.

**Status of each version, as verified:**

## 3. Children's Oncology Group Long-Term Follow-Up Guidelines v6.0 (October 2023)

**Full name:** Long-Term Follow-Up Guidelines for Survivors of Childhood, Adolescent, and Young Adult Cancers, Version 6.0, October 2023.
**URL read (full PDF downloaded, 211 pp., text-extracted):** https://www.survivorshipguidelines.org/pdf/2023/COG_LTFU_Guidelines_Only_v6.pdf
**Access level:** free/public.
**Nature:** a surveillance guideline, **not** a banded risk-categorization. It names exposures that cause gonadal late effects and lists "Cancer/Treatment factors ... that may increase risk." No high/intermediate/low fertility categories.

Gonadotoxic exposures covered (section numbers from the PDF):

- **Alkylating-agent exposure list (Sections 12–15):** "Classical Alkylating Agents: Busulfan, Carmustine (BCNU), Chlorambucil, Cyclophosphamide, Ifosfamide, Lomustine (CCNU), Mechlorethamine, Melphalan, Procarbazine, Thiotepa. Heavy Metals: Carboplatin, Cisplatin. Non-Classical Alkylators: Dacarbazine (DTIC), Temozolomide." Late effects: male — impaired spermatogenesis/oligospermia/azoospermia/infertility and testosterone deficiency; female — premature ovarian insufficiency, diminished ovarian reserve, infertility.
- **Male fertility risk factors (Section 13, verbatim):** "higher cumulative doses of alkylators (especially busulfan ≥600 mg/m2, cyclophosphamide ≥4 gm/m2, CED >4 gm/m2, ifosfamide ≥50 gm/m2), and cisplatin >488 mg/m2, combinations of alkylators, MOPP ≥3 cycles, cyclophosphamide as conditioning for HCT, in combination with radiation to abdomen/pelvis, testes, brain/cranium (neuroendocrine axis), or TBI, genitourinary surgery". Also: "Prepubertal status at treatment does not protect from gonadal injury in males."
- **Male Leydig/testosterone risk factors (Section 12, verbatim):** "...especially cyclophosphamide dose ≥20 gm/m2 or ifosfamide ≥60 gm/m2 ... cyclophosphamide as conditioning for HCT, in combination with radiation (to abdomen/pelvis, testes [especially dose ≥20 Gy], brain/cranium [neuroendocrine axis], or TBI), and unilateral orchiectomy".
- **Female risk factors (Sections 14–15, verbatim):** "Patient factors: Older age at treatment. Cancer/Treatment factors: Higher cumulative doses of alkylators or combinations of alkylators, combination with radiation to abdomen/pelvis, lumbar or sacral spine (from ovarian scatter), or brain/cranium (neuroendocrine axis), any alkylators combined with pelvic radiation or TBI". Also: "Females can typically maintain gonadal function at higher cumulative doses than males." No numeric female CED threshold is printed.
- **CED formula (Sections 14/15, verbatim):** "CED (mg/m2) = 1.0 (cumulative cyclophosphamide dose (mg/m2)) + 0.244 (cumulative ifosfamide dose (mg/m2)) + 0.857 (cumulative procarbazine dose (mg/m2)) + 14.286 (cumulative chlorambucil dose (mg/m2)) + 15 (cumulative BCNU dose (mg/m2)) + 16 (cumulative CCNU dose (mg/m2)) + 40 (cumulative melphalan dose (mg/m2)) + 50 (cumulative thiotepa dose (mg/m2)) + 100 (cumulative nitrogen mustard dose (mg/m2)) + 8.823 (cumulative busulfan dose (mg/m2))".
- **Testicular radiation (Section 91, verbatim):** "radiation dose to testes (up to 6 Gy azoospermia may be transient, ≥6 Gy azoospermia likely permanent and especially testicular dose ≥20 Gy), combination with alkylating agents, genitourinary surgery"; "fractionated small doses greater risk than single large doses". Leydig section 90: "testicular dose ≥12 Gy" flagged for hormonal dysfunction.
- **Female pelvic/TBI radiation (Sections 92–93, verbatim):** "Radiation dose ≥5 Gy if pubertal (especially ≥10 Gy), dose ≥10 Gy if prepubertal (especially ≥15 Gy), combination with alkylating agent chemotherapy, longer time since treatment, combination with cyclophosphamide conditioning for HCT". Exposure header includes "Pelvis, Spine (sacral, whole), TBI".
- **Cranial radiation (Sections 58–59, male/female):** Head/Brain or TBI → "Gonadotropin deficiency, LH and FSH deficiency"; risk factor: "Higher radiation dose, especially ≥30 Gy, may occur at lower doses with longer follow-up".
- **Surgery (Sections 135–146):** oophoropexy; unilateral oophorectomy (DOR/infertility); bilateral oophorectomy ("Loss of ovarian follicular pool, Infertility"); unilateral/partial orchiectomy; bilateral orchiectomy ("Azoospermia, Infertility"); pelvic surgery/cystectomy (male: "Retrograde ejaculation, Anejaculation, Obstructive azoospermia, Infertility"); hysterectomy.
- **HCT (p. 111):** transplant late effects are multifactorial, "including prior therapy for primary malignancy, intensity of transplant conditioning, stem cell product ... donor ...".

## 6. CRISP — Cancer Related Infertility Score Predictor (UCSD)

**Source read:** Nerb L, Yang E, Exume D, et al. "Development, Usability Testing, and Implementation Assessment of Cancer Related Infertility Score Predictor, an Online Cancer Related Infertility Risk Counseling Tool." J Adolesc Young Adult Oncol. 2023;12(6):843–850. URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10739786/ (open access). Live tool: www.oncofertilityrisk.com (not scraped; risk mappings per regimen are inside the tool, not published as a table in the paper).

- **Scope:** females only; "infertility risk data for 60 treatment regimens among 10 cancer types" (brain, breast, cervical, Hodgkin's lymphoma, intestinal, leukemia, non-Hodgkin lymphoma, ovarian, sarcoma, uterine).
- **Inputs:** patient age, cancer type, treatment regimen (optional patient name). No dose entry required up front; a CED calculator is included as a separate feature.
- **Bands (color-coded):** Low (green) ≤20%; Moderate (yellow) 21%–70%; High (red) >70%; Unknown (black) = no credible data.
- **Output:** "Reproductive Risk Summary" — color-coded risk estimate with a percentage, the clinical outcome described (amenorrhea, acute ovarian failure, infertility, or premature menopause), comparison risks in women without cancer, references, ASCO guideline pointers, date.
- **Relevance to us:** validates the exact input set we collect (age + cancer type + regimen, no doses) as sufficient for banded counseling; but its regimen→band mapping table is not published, so we cannot implement *from* CRISP — only cite it as precedent.

## 7. Synthesis — what we can implement from our inputs

Our collected inputs: anatomy (ovaries/testes), age, AYA cancer diagnosis, treatment modality (chemo / radiation + body region / surgery / HSCT), optional chemo drug names. **No doses, no cycle counts, no Gy, no CED.** Output is a named category, never a probability.

### Recommended framework

**Primary: the Livestrong/Fertile Hope risk charts (Section 1), supplemented by ESMO 2020 Table 3 (Section 4b) for female regimen+age rows, with SaveMyFertility (Section 5) as a corroborating derivative of Livestrong.** Rationale:

- Livestrong is the only framework whose rows are predominantly keyed to things a patient knows: named regimen (MOPP, BEACOPP, ABVD, CHOP, COP, BEP, AC, CMF/CEF/CAF, FOLFOX4, anthracycline+cytarabine), modality combination ("any alkylating agent + TBI", "+ pelvic radiation", "conditioning for HSCT"), body region of radiation, and age bands — mostly without requiring numbers.
- ESMO 2020 is the strongest peer-reviewed citation (Annals of Oncology, open access) and adds female age splits for CHOP (≥35 vs <35), DA-EPOCH, BEACOPP (≥30 vs <30), AC (≥40 vs <40), CMF/CEF/CAF/TAC (≥40 / 30–39 / <30), FOLFOX, BEP/EP, sarcoma multi-agent regimens, and explicit entries for ABVD, AML therapy, ALL therapy, radioactive iodine, bevacizumab.
- CRISP (Section 6) is precedent that age + cancer type + regimen without doses supports banded output, with "Unknown" as a first-class band.

**Category labels supported:** `High` / `Intermediate` / `Lower` / `Very low or no` / `Unknown` (Livestrong's five bands; ESMO uses four: High >80%, Intermediate 20–80%, Low <20%, Unknown). If we expose percentage glosses, note the definitions differ: Livestrong female High is ">70% of women develop amenorrhea"; ESMO High is ">80%"; CRISP High is ">70%". Safest: use the labels without percentages, or quote each source's own definition in the citation drawer.

### Which of our inputs each mapping uses

Usable directly:
- **Anatomy/sex** — every framework is sex-split.
- **Treatment modality:** HSCT/transplant conditioning → High (Livestrong both sexes; ESMO both sexes; SaveMyFertility both sexes — the single most consistent mapping across all sources). TBI → High (all sources). Pelvic radiation + any alkylator → High (Livestrong both; SMF both). Testicular radiation (any therapeutic dose context) and "radiotherapy to a field including the ovaries" → High (ESMO female EBRT >6 Gy; ASCO-lineage "radiotherapy to a field including the ovaries"; Livestrong female >6 Gy adult).
- **Drug names (no dose):** the alkylator list for the "alkylator + TBI/pelvic/testicular RT" and "alkylator conditioning for HSCT" high-risk rows: busulfan, carmustine (BCNU), cyclophosphamide, ifosfamide, lomustine (CCNU), melphalan, procarbazine (Livestrong row text), + chlorambucil, mechlorethamine, thiotepa (ESMO male list; COG classical-alkylator list). Procarbazine-containing regimens by name (MOPP, BEACOPP, COPP, MVPP, ChlVPP) map without doses when cycle count is unknown only via the conservative reading below.
- **Age (female):** required for CMF/CEF/CAF/TAC (<30 / 30–39 / ≥40), AC x4 (<40 / ≥40), BEACOPP escalated (<30 / ≥30), CHOP x6 (<35 / ≥35), DA-EPOCH x6 (<35 / ≥35), FOLFOX (≥40 vs ≤40 — note the published overlap at exactly 40), BEP/EP (<30), sarcoma regimens (<35), Livestrong cyclophosphamide rows (>40 / 30–40 / <20). Exact published wordings are quoted in Sections 1a, 4b, 5a.
- **Pubertal status (pediatric radiation):** Livestrong female "> 10 Gy in post-pubertal girls, > 15 Gy in pre-pubertal girls"; male "> 2.5 Gy in men, > 6 Gy in boys"; COG female "≥5 Gy if pubertal ... ≥10 Gy if prepubertal". All are dose-qualified, so pubertal status alone cannot band radiation — see fallbacks.
- **Surgery:** only COG (Sections 135–146: bilateral oophorectomy/orchiectomy = loss of fertility; unilateral = DOR/risk; cystectomy/pelvic surgery = ejaculatory/obstructive infertility) and SaveMyFertility men ("Surgical removal of one or both testicles or the pituitary gland" = High; pelvic surgery = Intermediate) name surgical exposures. Livestrong charts and ESMO tables do not band surgery (ESMO handles it as recommendations, not a table).

**Cannot use (dose/cycle-keyed rows):** every g/m², mg/m², Gy, CED, and cycle-count threshold: Livestrong cyclophosphamide 5/7.5 g/m² rows; MOPP >3 / BEACOPP >6 cycles; all radiation Gy bands (including cranial >40 Gy — a patient knows "radiation to the head," never the Gy); male cisplatin >400 mg/m² / carboplatin >2 g/m² / BEP 2–4 cycles; ESMO CED >5 g/m²; all COG thresholds (busulfan ≥600 mg/m², cyclophosphamide ≥4 g/m², CED >4 g/m², ifosfamide ≥50 g/m², cisplatin >488 mg/m², testicular ≥6/≥12/≥20 Gy, pelvic ≥5/≥10/≥15 Gy, cranial ≥30 Gy); ESMO female cycle counts (x2 vs x6–8 BEACOPP; x4 AC; x6 CMF etc.).

### Mandatory fallbacks to "Variable / unknown — depends on dose"

Because no source bands these exposures without a number, the tool must NOT assign a band for:
1. **Standalone cyclophosphamide / ifosfamide (or unspecified "chemo with an alkylating agent") without a named regimen, transplant, or concurrent pelvic/testicular RT/TBI** — risk is dose-dependent in every source (Livestrong bands it only at 5 vs 7.5 g/m²; COG at CED thresholds). Display: alkylating agents are the class "that does the most damage to fertility" (Livestrong men's live page) with risk depending on total dose → variable/unknown band + strong "ask your team for your cumulative dose / CED" prompt.
2. **Radiation to abdomen/pelvis, testes, or brain without dose** — direction is knowable (these are the fields all sources flag), magnitude is not (Livestrong intermediate vs high splits at 6/10/15 Gy female, 0.2/0.7/1–6/2.5/6 Gy male; cranial only banded at >40 Gy). Exception that stays assignable: TBI (always High) and "alkylating agent + pelvic/testicular radiation" (High in Livestrong/SMF regardless of dose).
3. **Cisplatin/carboplatin in males without cycle count or dose** (Livestrong intermediate rows are dose/cycle-keyed). In females, "Protocols containing cisplatin" is Intermediate per Livestrong women (no dose qualifier) — assignable.
4. **Procarbazine regimens (MOPP/BEACOPP) without cycle count** — Livestrong's High rows are ">3 cycles"/">6 cycles". ESMO lets escalated BEACOPP 6–8 cycles band by age, and 2 cycles = Low. Without cycles: either variable/unknown, or High with explicit "assuming a full course" caveat — a product decision; do not silently assume.
5. **Anything not named by any source** (e.g., blinatumomab, inotuzumab, CAR-T, checkpoint inhibitors beyond "immunotherapy") → Unknown. ESMO explicitly bands "Immunotherapy" and "most targeted therapies" as Unknown risk — cite that rather than inventing.

### What ASCO contributes
No currently public ASCO document supplies an extractable 2025 risk table: JCO-24-02782 is paywalled (403), the COG endorsement PDF reproduces recommendations only, and the 2013 full text contains no risk table. The ASCO lineage reaches us only via the 2006 Lee tables as adapted in ESMO Table 2 and reproduced in Zavras 2016. Cite "ASCO 2006, as adapted by ESMO 2020" — do not cite "ASCO 2025" for any band until someone with access checks its tables.

### Biggest limitation (one sentence)
Every framework's fine structure is dose- and cycle-keyed, and our patients know neither — so the tool can confidently band only (a) modality combinations (HSCT conditioning, TBI, alkylator + pelvic/testicular radiation), (b) named regimens with the patient's age for females, and (c) explicitly listed low/unknown-risk agents, while standalone alkylators, platinum agents in males, and any dose-unspecified radiation field must land in an honest "variable/unknown — depends on dose" band with a prompt to ask the care team for cumulative dose (CED) or radiation dose.
