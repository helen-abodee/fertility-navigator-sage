## D2 — Ability to Have Children: Fertility Issues (City of Hope info sheet, 2017 sources)

**What it covers:** One-page overview of how chemo and radiation affect fertility, plus resources and financial notes. Sources are cancer.gov pages dated June 2017, so treat every claim here as the **oldest** tier of evidence.

**Facts it supports:**
- Radiation and chemo "may cause temporary or permanent infertility"; factors include sex, age at treatment, type and dose of radiation/chemo, single vs. multiple therapies, and time since treatment [D2, p.1].
- Chemo: age matters and "recovery improves the longer the patient is off chemotherapy." Drugs shown to affect fertility: busulfan, melphalan, cyclophosphamide, cisplatin, chlorambucil, mustine, carmustine, lomustine, vinblastine, cytarabine, procarbazine. In women over 40, adjuvant endocrine therapy increases risk of permanent loss of menstrual periods [D2, p.1].
- Radiation: the amount of radiation directed to the testes or ovaries matters; in women over 40, infertility may occur at lower doses. Lead shields can protect the testes; women may undergo surgery to move ovaries out of the radiation field (ovarian transposition, one sentence only) [D2, p.1].
- Options "may include freezing sperm, eggs, or ovarian tissue before cancer treatment"; discuss with the doctor before treatment; a counselor or fertility specialist can help with decisions [D2, p.1].
- Resource pointers: Oncofertility Consortium, Fertile Hope (fertilehope.org), ASCO (asco.org), Livestrong [D2, p.2]. NOTE: Fertile Hope is listed as a live organization; this is outdated (see GAPS).
- Local resources listed "as a convenience… do not constitute an endorsement by City of Hope": California Cryobank; Huntington Reproductive Center Medical Group (havingbabies.com, i.e., HRC) [D2, p.2].
- Financial: "procedures such as sperm banking, egg freezing and embryo freezing are usually not covered" by insurance; coverage varies, ask your insurer; financial assistance exists (names Fertile Hope Sharing Hope) [D2, p.2]. **CONFLICTS with D4 for California; see GAPS.md.**

## D3 — Fertility Preservation Resources in Oncology (CancerCon 2026 take-home)

**What it covers:** A provider-facing link list: education programs, guidelines, CED calculators, insurance resources, and patient financial-assistance organizations. Almost all substance lives behind links; the sheet itself asserts few facts.

**Facts it supports:**
- Framing: fertility preservation conversations "should happen with every patient of reproductive age" (quoting Eleonora Teplinsky); cancer affects emotional wellbeing, relationships, identity and family-building, not just fertility biology [D3, p.1].
- Named guideline bodies for FP: ASCO (guideline update), COG supportive care, ASRM committee opinion, Alliance for Fertility Preservation [D3, p.1].
- UC San Diego Moores Cancer Center publishes a Cyclophosphamide Equivalent Dose (CED) calculator and a Cancer-Related Infertility Score Predictor (CRISP) [D3, p.1]. (Links only; no formulas included — the risk module stays blocked on Hannah's document.)
- Insurance: Alliance for Fertility Preservation maintains a state laws/legislation map and insurance coverage info [D3, p.2].
- Financial assistance organizations (names + one-line descriptors only): Chick Mission Hope Grant (young women diagnosed with cancer), Expect Miracles Foundation Family Building Grant (survivors), Fertility Within Reach (grant/discount list), Livestrong Fertility, ReUnite (discounted meds), Team Maggie (ages 15-37, preservation costs), Verna's Purse (shipping and storage costs), Worth The Wait (fertility treatment, adoption, surrogacy), Facing Hereditary Cancer EMPOWERED [D3, p.2].

## D4 — Fertility Preservation Benefit Mandates Toolkit (UCSD + AFP, July 2025)

**What it covers:** The most substantive document. Insurance mandate landscape, costs, covered-service definitions, philanthropic resources with eligibility detail, and a patient guide for verifying benefits (with CPT codes, scripts, and a California appeal letter). This is our primary source for cost and insurance content.

**Facts it supports:**

*Definitions and costs*
- Definition: FP is preserving reproductive cells (sperm, eggs, reproductive tissue) to protect them from being harmed/destroyed by medical intervention such as cancer treatment (per ASCO 2025) [D4, p.2-3].
- FP vs. infertility treatment distinction (proactive preservation vs. treating existing infertility); both use many of the same services [D4, p.3].
- Costs (per Alliance for Fertility Preservation, 2024): average one egg-freezing cycle in the US = **$12,400**, excluding medications (**~$5,000**); sperm test/analyze/freeze = **$500-$1,000**; storage of eggs or sperm = **$200-$500 per year**; plus office visits, diagnostic/genetic testing, and indirect costs like time away from work (Weigel et al., 2020) [D4, p.3].
- Patients often pay out of pocket even when insured (Weigel et al., 2020); FP patients experience significant financial toxicity (Meernik, 2022); upfront cash requirements are a significant barrier [D4, p.3, 9-10].

*Comprehensive FP service lists (Su et al., 2024)* [D4, p.9]
- Egg/embryo (IVF) route: specialist consult; infectious disease testing, ultrasounds, hormone testing, egg retrieval; egg or embryo freeze, storage, embryo creation/culture, biopsy and genetic testing; fertility medications.
- Ovarian tissue route: consult; infectious disease testing; oophorectomy or ovarian biopsy; ovarian tissue cryopreservation; storage.
- Sperm route: consult; infectious disease testing; sperm retrieval procedures; sperm cryopreservation; storage; medications to facilitate obtaining ejaculated sperm.
- Testicular tissue route: consult; infectious disease testing; testicular aspiration or biopsy; cryopreservation; storage.

*Insurance mandates*
- 20 states + DC have FP benefit mandates (CT and RI first, 2017); state-by-state coverage matrix as of June 1, 2025 [D4, p.4, 12-13, 33-34].
- **California row (as of June 1, 2025): commercial fully-insured = Yes; Medicaid (Medi-Cal) = No.** All coverage specifics for CA are "NS" (not specified) in the mandate matrix; no cycle limits, parity, or prior-auth details specified [D4, p.12, 33].
- Plan types subject to state mandates: fully-insured commercial, ACA Marketplace, Medicaid/CHIP (varies by state). Not subject: self-insured employer plans (unlikely to win appeal), Medicare (does not cover FP as of Oct 2024), military plans (cover FP for active service members as of May 2024, dependents ineligible), federal employee plans (FP required by federal statute) [D4, p.7-8, 31-32].
- Patients can appeal denials; appeals typically take 30-60 days non-urgent, **4 days for urgent cases**; a physician can submit the appeal [D4, p.35].
- California specifics (appeal letter): the DMHC recognizes FP for cancer patients at risk of iatrogenic infertility as a covered benefit under the Knox-Keene Act, detailed by the passage of **California Senate Bill 600 in 2019**; the remaining question in an appeal is medical necessity; published Independent Medical Reviews have sided with similarly situated patients [D4, p.36].
- Benefit verification script: the exact phone script distinguishing "medically indicated fertility preservation / iatrogenic infertility" from "general infertility benefits," with CPT code tables (egg retrieval 58970, oocyte cryo 89337, embryo cryo 89258, ovarian tissue cryo 89398, sperm cryo 89259, sperm storage 89343, etc.), lifetime-maximum questions, in-network clinic search, out-of-network and out-of-pocket questions, and pharmacy benefit drug list (Gonal-F, Follistim, Menopur, etc.) [D4, p.20-29].
- Glossary of insurance terms in plain language (deductible, co-insurance, prior authorization, self-insured vs fully-insured, IMR, etc.) [D4, p.38-39].

*Philanthropic resources with eligibility detail* [D4, p.14-16]
- Allyson Whitney Foundation: US citizen, age 16-36, rare cancer, active treatment; covers egg retrieval, sperm banking; application must be requested by a facility.
- Chick Mission: cancer patients pre-treatment; **available only in CA, CO, IL, NJ, NY, TX**; screening before application.
- Expect Miracles SAMFund: US resident, age 21-39; notification 10-11 weeks after deadline.
- Fertility Within Reach: patient and clinic complete application together.
- Livestrong Fertility: in the US, cancer diagnosis, oncologist attests treatment poses fertility risk.
- NMDP: sickle cell disease, pre-transplant; covers egg retrieval/cryo, embryo work, ovarian tissue freezing, sperm banking, TESE, storage, meds.
- ReUnite Oncofertility: discounted meds (Follistim AQ, ganirelix acetate, Pregnyl).
- Team Maggie's Dream: US citizen, age 15-37.
- Verna's Purse (ReproTech): discounted long-term storage; requires infectious disease blood testing.
- Walgreens Specialty Pharmacy: new cancer diagnosis, female; donated Ferring products, 2 FP cycles.
- Worth the Wait: cancer diagnosis, age 13-39, not yet a parent; covers egg/embryo/sperm preservation, FET, storage, adoption, surrogacy; **grants $500-$2,500**.
- Plus a general infertility table (BabyQuest, Hasidah, Hope for Fertility, Men Having Babies, Nest Egg, Starfish, etc.) — mostly relevant to survivors pursuing treatment later, with eligibility caveats (several require an infertility diagnosis or marriage).

**Use in app:** primary source for Part 3 cost/insurance content, the "questions to ask" generator (benefit verification script), financial aid directory, and the insurance branch of the click-through. Attribute as "UC San Diego Moores Cancer Center and Alliance for Fertility Preservation toolkit, updated July 31, 2025."
