The literature behind the thinking-it-through path

What was found, what each source contributes, and what we may legally reuse. Full extraction with verbatim items: content/sources/parenthood-decision-aids.md in the repo. Retrieved November 2, 2026.

Ottawa Personal Decision Guide our backbone · free with attribution

O'Connor, Stacey, Jacobsen · Ottawa Hospital Research Institute · decisionaid.ohri.ca

The most-used general tool for any hard health decision, in four steps: clarify the decision, explore what matters (a 0 to 5 "how much it matters" rating with "unsure" as a legal option), identify support needs, and plan next steps. It is the source of our slider exercise and of the pressure check, asked verbatim in the guide: "Are you feeling pressured by this person?" Free to use and prefill with citation for non-commercial use, which is why it's our structure.

BEFORE decision aid paper CC BY · tool wording needs permission

Speller, Baxter et al. · BMC Medical Informatics and Decision Making, 2019 · fertilityaid.rethinkbreastcancer.com

"Begin Exploring Fertility Options, Risks and Expectations," a Canadian aid for young women with breast cancer. Contributes the two-clock framing (preserve now vs parent someday), "I am not sure yet" as a checkbox outcome, wait-and-see pros and cons, and two-directional regret items. We adapted the structure and cite the paper; its exact tool wording would need written approval from St. Michael's Hospital to reuse, so we didn't.

Cancer, Fertility and Me paper CC BY · booklet quotes attributed

Jones et al. · Frontiers in Oncology, 2022 · booklet PDF (Leeds Beckett)

The most AYA-relevant validated aid: tested in women 16 to 43 across cancer types. Contributes the 1 to 4 "What matters most to you?" values table, a Definitely-not-to-Definitely scale with "Unsure" at the midpoint, and the plain statement "There is no 'right' or 'wrong' decision." Its developers deliberately rejected case-study stories as biasing, which shaped our no-stories choice.

CDC Reproductive Life Plan public domain · adapt freely

CDC / HHS Office of Population Affairs · toolkit

Source of our "plans are allowed to change" screen: the federal guidance is built on the observation that people's reproductive plans change, and asks rather than assumes. Public domain, so it's the one source we adapted wholesale.

Also reviewed

  • FAST (Family-centered Adolescent Sperm banking values Tool): the only adolescent-specific values tool found; its items are unpublished, but its teen-and-parent compare-answers pattern is noted for a future caregiver feature.
  • Peate et al. breast-cancer fertility decision aid: strong trial evidence that decision aids reduce decisional conflict; wording would need permission.
  • Childbearing Motivations Scale (Guedes et al.): we borrowed its published motivation domains (relational, personal, social) for the "what parenthood could mean" prompts, not its licensed items.
  • IPDAS standards: the international criteria for unbiased decision aids, source of our equipoise rule that the tool never leans a direction, and that "unsure" is a resting place, not a failure state.

How the 8 screens map to sources

  • Two decisions, two clocks → Ottawa step 1; BEFORE structure
  • Locate yourself, torn is legal → Ottawa "unsure"; BEFORE "not sure yet"
  • What parenthood could mean → CMS domains; FAST health-worry item
  • Plans are allowed to change → CDC, public domain
  • What preserving does and doesn't promise → BEFORE and CFM honest-uncertainty lines
  • More than one road (donor, surrogacy, adoption, fostering) → BEFORE; CFM
  • What matters sliders (0 to 5, unscored) → Ottawa step 2; CFM values table
  • Who to talk with, pressure check, deciding later on purpose → Ottawa steps 3 and 4; BEFORE wait-and-see
