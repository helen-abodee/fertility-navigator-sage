On imagery of children: my honest take

Skip it. The decision-aid standards we built on (IPDAS) require not nudging a direction, and the Cancer-Fertility-and-Me team explicitly rejected emotionally loaded material as biasing. Baby imagery leans "yes" on a page whose whole job is equipoise, and it can wound the person who ends up unable to, or chooses not to. What shipped instead: each reflection step now opens with a large icon in our own warm line style (calendar for the two clocks, heart for meaning, compass for locating yourself), which gives the visual anchor you wanted without the thumb on the scale. If you want more art, the right move is abstract illustration in this same language (seeds, paths, horizons), not photography.

One-tap "Send to my care team", no SMS ever

Built and flag-gated. The Compare tab

New tab between Options and Paying for it. Pick up to three options; a matrix grades six considerations, each cell carrying words, never color alone: time before treatment, what it involves, upfront cost, ongoing cost, evidence status, decisions needed now. Three honest levels (easier / in between / bigger lift, plus unknown for unpublished prices), with a one-line summary per option ("Sperm banking: the fastest, simplest, cheapest option on this list"). Every row cites the same sources as the option's own page.
