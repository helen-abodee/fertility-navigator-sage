The HIPAA line, made very clear

The one-sentence rule

HIPAA binds providers and anyone handling patient data on a provider's behalf. It does not bind patients: a patient texting their own progress to a social worker can never violate HIPAA.

What we can do with zero HIPAA exposure (and did)

  • Everything shipped so far: answers on-device, codes made on the patient's phone and sent by the patient, the clinician board decoding them in the clinician's own browser.
  • Your care-team directory idea: yes, safely. A built-in picker of the program's social workers is just public staff contact info, like a hospital website footer. The patient taps their person instead of typing a number; sending still happens from their own phone. Shipped now with clearly labeled sample entries, because the real constraint isn't HIPAA, it's our unbranded rule: listing City of Hope staff needs their written permission.
  • What the social worker keeps after receiving a text falls under City of Hope's existing compliance, not ours.

What would trigger HIPAA

The moment any server we run receives identifiable patient info to deliver it to the care team (a send button routing through our backend, a database linking patients to social workers with progress attached, push notifications of stage changes), we become a business associate. Pseudonyms don't escape it. Then the checklist is: a BAA with City of Hope (which requires an operating legal entity on our side), HIPAA-eligible hosting with its own BAA, encryption in transit and at rest, real staff authentication, audit logs, minimum-necessary design, a retention policy, breach notification within 60 days, and City of Hope's security review. Not prohibitive, just a sequencing decision. Full brief: docs/HIPAA-BRIEF.md.
