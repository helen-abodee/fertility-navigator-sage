The honest finding first: cream and serif aren't the sin. Oura's brand ground is literally warm cream with a serif display, and One Medical chose its serif on purpose. What reads "AI-generated" is indecision: cream washed over everything, a default-tracked generic serif, and a green accent doing trust work that real health brands do with evidence (study counts, guideline badges, institution names). All three directions below fix the execution; they differ in temperament.

Why 3 is the recommendation

It cures the exact complaint (all-over cream, generic-feeling type, green-does-everything) without discarding what's working: the warm section-2 mood your testers will meet at the most emotional question, the milestone system, and the existing content design. Direction 1 is the safe pick if you want maximum clinical credibility for the Dr. Su and mentor meetings; direction 2 is the memorable pick but commits the whole product to a dark identity. All three keep WCAG AA and the anti-anxiety rules.

Clinic pictures: the licensing reality

  • Scraping clinic sites' photos is out: their photos are copyrighted, and we'd be burning relationships with the exact clinics we want as partners.
  • Google Places photos are technically allowed in third-party apps, but require per-photo attribution, a Google Maps linkback, no caching, and they're unart-directed user shots (parking lots, waiting rooms), which would undermine the redesign.
  • No fertility clinic we checked publishes a press kit.
  • The credible plan: ship designed clinic cards now (monogram mark + a small live map thumbnail with attribution + the structured facts: distance, oncofertility services, tap-to-call), and send all 17 clinics a short permission request for 2 or 3 photos. What changes first (impact order)

  1. Ground and surfaces: paper-white base, cream demoted to section bands (kills the biggest "AI" tell in one move).
  2. Type discipline: tighten the display face's tracking and size jumps; mono or tabular figures for every number.
  3. Evidence as a visual element: small "UCSD CRISP · ASCO 2025" chips where trust matters, instead of green doing all the work.
  4. Accent swap per the chosen direction; section moods re-tuned to it.
  5. Clinic cards redesigned (monogram + map thumb + facts), photo permissions letter out.
  6. Micro-polish: consistent corner radii, hairline borders, one shadow level.
