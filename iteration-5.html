The risk calculation, laid out

Ten rules, checked top to bottom; the first match wins. Everything the published frameworks key to dose or cycle count honestly lands in "depends on your dose" instead of guessing.

Care-team tracking: built what's safe, designed the rest

  • Shipped now: "Where I am with this." A patient-held checklist on the results page (learned my risk, talked with my care team, checked coverage, contacted a clinic, had a consult, started preservation), with two equal endings: "I completed preservation" and "I decided not to preserve." It saves on-device and prints into the summary, so a social worker sees structured status the moment a patient shares it. No scores, no percentages, by test.
  • Designed for Estella: check-in codes. docs/DATA-ARCHITECTURE.md lays out three tiers. The recommended v2: the social worker generates a code per patient, the patient opts in, and ONLY stage checkmarks (never answers, diagnosis, or risk inputs) sync to a small staff dashboard. Honest catch for that conversation: even stage-only data linked to a code is PHI, so it needs a HIPAA business associate agreement and City of Hope sign-off before anything is built. The full platform (tier 3, Dyad-shaped on Supabase's HIPAA tier) stays on ice until the program commits.

Egg and sperm health, Dyad-style

A new results section, "Everyday things that affect sperm and eggs," opens with "Information, not homework. Nothing here is a goal." Ten facts, every one sourced to ASRM fact sheets or Cleveland Clinic urology pages, each teaching the mechanism: sperm take about 2.5 months to develop; testes run cooler on purpose, so heat matters; testosterone and steroids shut sperm production down; your cannabis fact, stated with its honest caveat (mechanism via THC and testosterone; clinic-population studies are mixed); smoking speeds up egg loss that can't be undone; and a debunk card (supplements and after-sex positions have no evidence) to save people money and worry. Anatomy-gated like everything else.

And Dyad's enforcement came with the content: the banned-phrase test is ported and expanded, so CI now fails on any wellness platitude ("be gentle with yourself", "self-care", "listen to your body"), any fertility-improvement claim, any urgency phrase, any em dash, across every string in the app including the new facts.
